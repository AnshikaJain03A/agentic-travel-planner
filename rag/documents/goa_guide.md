# Goa Travel Guide

Goa is India's smallest state, famous for its beaches, Portuguese-era architecture,
nightlife, seafood and laid-back culture. It is split into North Goa (livelier,
party-focused) and South Goa (quieter, more upscale and scenic).

## Best Time to Visit
The peak season runs from November to February with pleasant, dry weather (around
22-32 C) - ideal for beaches and water sports. March to May is hot and humid.
The monsoon (June to September) brings heavy rain, lush greenery, lower prices and
fewer crowds, but many beach shacks and water-sports operators close.

## Top Attractions
- Baga, Calangute and Anjuna beaches (North Goa) for nightlife and water sports.
- Palolem and Agonda beaches (South Goa) for calm, scenic relaxation.
- Basilica of Bom Jesus and Se Cathedral in Old Goa (UNESCO World Heritage).
- Fort Aguada and Chapora Fort for sunset views.
- Dudhsagar Waterfalls, best seen just after the monsoon.
- Anjuna Flea Market (Wednesdays) and Saturday Night Market at Arpora.

## Hidden Gems
- Divar Island, a quiet island reached by ferry, with old churches and paddy fields.
- Butterfly Beach near Palolem, accessible by boat.
- Cabo de Rama Fort, an uncrowded clifftop fort in South Goa.
- Sao Jacinto Island for birdwatching and quiet walks.
- Fontainhas, the Latin Quarter in Panaji, full of colourful heritage houses.

## Food
Goan cuisine blends Indian and Portuguese flavours. Must-try dishes: fish curry
rice, prawn balchao, chicken cafreal, pork vindaloo, sorpotel, bebinca dessert and
fresh seafood at beach shacks. Feni is the local cashew/coconut spirit.

## Budget Tips
- Rent a scooter (about INR 300-500/day) to get around cheaply.
- Eat at local "thali" places and beach shacks rather than resort restaurants.
- Visit in the shoulder season (November or February) for cheaper stays.
- South Goa guesthouses are often cheaper and calmer than North Goa resorts.

## Safety and Practical Tips
- Swim only in flagged areas; be cautious of strong currents, especially in monsoon.
- Drinking and driving is strictly enforced; always wear a helmet on scooters.
- Carry cash for shacks and markets; many small vendors do not take cards.
- Respect church and temple dress codes (cover shoulders and knees).
- Emergency: dial 112 (all-India), 108 for ambulance, 100 for police.
